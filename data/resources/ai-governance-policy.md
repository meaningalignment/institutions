# AI governance & policy

How states, labs, and international bodies oversee advanced AI, through regulation, standards, audits, and new institutions.

## Selected papers

- [The Agentic Web Requires New Normative Infrastructure](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10711)
  - Authors: Cameron Pattison, Matthew Boulos, Noam Kolt, Changbai Li, Tiziano Piccardi, Seth Lazar
  - Year: 2026
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-rights, community-protocols, national-rights
  - Summary: Makes the case for laws, norms, and practices that distinguish appropriately delegated agents from malicious automation on the web.

- [Distributional AGI safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16856)
  - Authors: Nenad Tomašev, Matija Franklin, Julian Jacobs, Sébastien Krier, Simon Osindero
  - Year: 2025
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: national-rights, national-incentives, global-incentives
  - Summary: Frames the distribution of benefits, risks, and decision power as a central part of AGI safety rather than a downstream policy question.

- [Regulatory Markets: The Future of AI Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.04914)
  - Authors: Gillian Hadfield, Jack Clark
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: Jurimetrics
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-incentives, national-expertise, global-incentives
  - Summary: Proposes markets for privately supplied regulatory services under democratically set public objectives, aiming to combine accountability with regulatory innovation.

## Work in the field

- [From Democracies to Autocracies: How AI Systems Enable Authoritarianism by Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.17286)
  - Authors: Jeba Sania, Marta Ziosi, Fazl Barez
  - Year: 2026
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: national-rights, national-protocols, national-expertise
  - Summary: Maps six deployed AI systems across regimes to identify design and governance features—data centralisation, regulatory gaps, weak oversight—that enable authoritarian control.

- [Legal Infrastructure for Transformative AI Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01474)
  - Authors: Gillian Hadfield
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: PNAS
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-protocols, national-rights, global-protocols
  - Summary: Shifts attention from choosing rules to building legal systems that can generate and implement rules for frontier models and autonomous agents.

- [Racing to Ruin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27638)
  - Authors: Drew Fudenberg, Andrew Koh
  - Year: 2026
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: global-incentives, national-incentives
  - Summary: Models an R&D race in which advancing the frontier raises the risk of catastrophe for all, and bounds how far competition pushes firms past what a single developer would choose.

- [AGI, Governments, and Free Societies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05710)
  - Authors: Justin B. Bullock, Samuel Hammond, Seb Krier
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: Springer
  - Type: Book chapter
  - Cells: national-rights, national-protocols, global-rights
  - Summary: Uses the narrow-corridor framework to argue AGI pushes states toward either despotic surveillance or eroded legitimacy, and proposes institutional safeguards for each failure mode.

- [AI Governance through Markets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17755)
  - Authors: Philip Moreira Tomei, Rupal Jain, Matija Franklin
  - Year: 2025
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: national-incentives, global-incentives, national-expertise
  - Summary: Argues insurance, auditing, procurement, and due diligence markets tie AI risk to financial risk, complementing regulation with incentives for responsible development.

- [Characterizing AI Agents for Alignment and Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.21848)
  - Authors: Atoosa Kasirzadeh, Iason Gabriel
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: Nature
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-rights, national-rights, global-protocols
  - Summary: Grades AI agents along autonomy, efficacy, goal complexity, and generality, building agentic profiles that map each type to its distinct governance challenges.

- [Incident Analysis for AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.14231)
  - Authors: Carson Ezell, Xavier Roberts-Gaal, Alan Chan
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: AIES 2025
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-protocols, group-protocols, national-rights
  - Summary: Proposes a taxonomy of system, contextual, and cognitive causes of agent incidents, and specifies the logs and documentation investigators need to attribute them.

- [The Stories We Govern By: AI, Risk, and the Power of Imaginaries](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/36686)
  - Authors: Ninell Oldenburg, Gleb Papyshev
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: AIES 2025
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-norms, global-norms, national-expertise
  - Summary: Examines how competing imaginaries of AI risk shape which governance arrangements become thinkable, and which policy alternatives are foreclosed as a result.

- [Automatic Authorities: Power and AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05990)
  - Authors: Seth Lazar
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: MIT Press
  - Type: Book chapter
  - Cells: community-rights, national-rights, national-norms
  - Summary: Analyzes automated systems as exercises of power and argues that legitimate use requires procedural authority, not only beneficial outcomes.

- [Black-Box Access is Insufficient for Rigorous AI Audits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.14446)
  - Authors: Stephen Casper, Carson Ezell, Charlotte Siegmann, Noam Kolt, Taylor Lynn Curtis, Benjamin Bucknall, Andreas Haupt, Kevin Wei, Jérémy Scheurer, Marius Hobbhahn, et al.
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: FAccT 2024
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-expertise, global-expertise, national-protocols
  - Summary: Argues that auditing AI systems demands white-box and development-process access, and that audits should disclose the level of access auditors actually received.

- [Personhood credentials: Artificial intelligence and the value of privacy-preserving tools to distinguish who is real online](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.07892)
  - Authors: Steven Adler, Zoë Hitzig, Shrey Jain, Catherine Brewer, Wayne Chang, Renée DiResta, Eddy Lazzarin, Sean McGregor, Wendy Seltzer, Divya Siddarth, et al.
  - Year: 2024
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, community-rights, global-protocols
  - Summary: Develops privacy-preserving credentials that let people prove they are human online while limiting scalable deception by automated actors.

- [Robust Technology Regulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17398)
  - Authors: Andrew Koh, Sivakorn Sanguanmoo
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: EC 2026
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-incentives, global-incentives, national-protocols
  - Summary: Derives an adaptive sandbox that taxes activity beyond a moving threshold, guaranteeing payoffs against adversarial agent learning without requiring advance commitment.

- [Frontier AI Regulation: Managing Emerging Risks to Public Safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.03718)
  - Authors: Markus Anderljung, Joslyn Barnhart, Anton Korinek, Jade Leung, Cullen O'Keefe, Jess Whittlestone, Shahar Avin, Miles Brundage, Justin Bullock, Duncan Cass-Beggs, Ben Chang, Tantum Collins, Tim Fist, Gillian Hadfield, Alan Hayes, Lewis Ho, Sara Hooker, Eric Horvitz, Noam Kolt, Jonas Schuett, Yonadav Shavit, Divya Siddarth, Robert Trager, Kevin Wolf
  - Year: 2023
  - Type: Report
  - Cells: national-protocols, national-rights, national-expertise
  - Summary: Sets out standards, registration, reporting, and enforcement as building blocks for governing frontier AI development.

- [Harms from Increasingly Agentic Algorithmic Systems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.10329)
  - Authors: Alan Chan, Rebecca Salganik, Alva Markelius, Chris Pang, Nitarshan Rajkumar, Dmitrii Krasheninnikov, Lauro Langosco, Zhonghao He, Yawen Duan, Micah Carroll, et al.
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: FAccT 2023
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-rights, group-protocols, global-norms
  - Summary: Identifies underspecification, directness of impact, goal-directedness, and long-term planning as the properties that raise system agency, and maps the harms each enables.

- [Toward Trustworthy AI Development: Mechanisms for Supporting Verifiable Claims](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.07213)
  - Authors: Miles Brundage, Shahar Avin, Jasmine Wang, Haydn Belfield, Gretchen Krueger, Gillian Hadfield, Heidy Khlaaf, Jingying Yang, Helen Toner, Ruth Fong, et al.
  - Year: 2020
  - Type: Report
  - Cells: global-protocols, national-protocols, global-expertise
  - Summary: Proposes institutional, software, and hardware mechanisms through which AI developers can make claims about safety, security, and fairness that outsiders can verify.

- [The Role of Cooperation in Responsible AI Development](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.04534)
  - Authors: Amanda Askell, Miles Brundage, Gillian Hadfield
  - Year: 2019
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: community-norms, national-incentives, global-protocols
  - Summary: Treats responsible AI development as a collective-action problem among firms and identifies conditions and strategies that can support cooperation.

- [European Union regulations on algorithmic decision-making and a "right to explanation"](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.08813)
  - Authors: Bryce Goodman, Seth Flaxman
  - Year: 2016
  - Venue: AI Magazine
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-rights, dyadic-rights, national-expertise
  - Summary: Reads the GDPR's restrictions on automated individual decision-making as creating a right to explanation, and traces what that demand asks of algorithm designers.
