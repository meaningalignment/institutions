# Deliberative democracy

How groups reach decisions they can stand behind, and how AI can support deliberation at scale without steering it.

## Selected papers

- [AI can help humans find common ground in democratic deliberation](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq2852)
  - Authors: Michael Henry Tessler, Michiel A. Bakker, Daniel Jarrett, Hannah Sheahan, Martin J. Chadwick, Raphael Koster, Georgina Evans, Lucy Campbell-Gillingham, Tessa C. Collins, David C. Parkes, Matthew Botvinick, Christopher Summerfield
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Science
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-preferences, community-preferences, community-norms
  - Summary: Tests an AI mediator that drafts group statements and helps participants with conflicting political views identify shared ground.

- [What are human values, and how do we align AI to them?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10636)
  - Authors: Oliver Klingefjord, Ryan Lowe, Joe Edelman
  - Year: 2024
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: group-preferences, community-preferences, national-preferences
  - Summary: Elicits the considerations behind people's choices rather than ratings, and reconciles them across a population into a moral graph that can serve as an alignment target.

## Work in the field

- [DeliberationBench: A Normative Benchmark for the Influence of Large Language Models on Users' Views](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10018)
  - Authors: Luke Hewitt, Maximilian Kroner Dale, Paul de Font-Reaulx
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: IASEAI 2026
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-preferences, community-preferences, dyadic-preferences
  - Summary: Benchmarks how far six frontier language models shift 4,088 participants' policy views, using Deliberative Polling data as a standard for democratically legitimate influence.

- [Tackling civic participation challenges with emerging technologies: Beyond the hype](https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2025/04/tackling-civic-participation-challenges-with-emerging-technologies_bbe2a7f5/ec2ca9a2-en.pdf)
  - Authors: Mauricio Mejia, Paulina Boéchat, Eloise Gabadou
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: OECD
  - Type: Report
  - Cells: national-protocols, community-protocols, national-preferences
  - Summary: Assesses how AI, blockchain, and virtual reality can lower barriers to citizen participation and make deliberative processes more intelligible and accountable to governments.

- [Using Collective Dialogues and AI to Find Common Ground Between Israeli and Palestinian Peacebuilders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01769)
  - Authors: Andrew Konya, Luke Thorburn, Wasim Almasri, Oded Adomi Leshem, Ariel D. Procaccia, Lisa Schirch, Michiel A. Bakker
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: FAccT 2025
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-preferences, community-preferences, global-thick-commitments, global-norms
  - Summary: Applies language models, social choice methods, and collective dialogues with 138 peacebuilders to produce shared statements endorsed by at least 84 percent on each side.

- [AI and the Future of Digital Public Squares](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09988)
  - Authors: Beth Goldberg, Diana Acosta-Navas, Michiel Bakker, Ian Beacock, Matt Botvinick, Prateek Buch, Renée DiResta, Nandika Donthi, Nathanael Fast, Ravi Iyer, et al.
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Collective Intelligence
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: community-protocols, national-preferences, community-norms
  - Summary: Draws on consultations with over seventy experts to assess collective dialogue, bridging, community moderation, and proof-of-humanity systems as language-model applications for online civic discourse.

- [Collective Constitutional AI: Aligning a Language Model with Public Input](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.07814)
  - Authors: Saffron Huang, Divya Siddarth, Liane Lovitt, Thomas I. Liao, Esin Durmus, Alex Tamkin, Deep Ganguli
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: FAccT 2024
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: community-preferences, national-preferences, national-rights
  - Summary: Builds and tests a multi-stage process for sourcing public principles and integrating them into a language model constitution.

- [Enabling the Digital Democratic Revival: A Research Program for Digital Democracy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16863)
  - Authors: Davide Grossi, Ulrike Hahn, Michael Mäs, Andreas Nitsche, Jan Behrens, Niclas Boehmer, Markus Brill, Ulle Endriss, Umberto Grandi, Adrian Haret, et al.
  - Year: 2024
  - Type: Report
  - Cells: national-protocols, community-protocols, national-preferences
  - Summary: Sets out a multi-method research agenda for digital democracy technology, spanning voting rules, deliberation platforms, and empirical evaluation of democratic quality.

- [Large Language Models, and LLM-Based Agents, Should Be Used to Enhance the Digital Public Sphere](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12123)
  - Authors: Seth Lazar, Luke Thorburn, Tian Jin, Luca Belli
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Inquiry
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: community-preferences, national-preferences, community-protocols
  - Summary: Argues that LLM-based recommenders could replace current attention-allocation machinery by inferring reflective user goals, and sets out the technical obstacles to doing so.

- [Bridging Systems: Open Problems for Countering Destructive Divisiveness across Ranking, Recommenders, and Governance](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.09976)
  - Authors: Aviv Ovadya, Luke Thorburn
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: Knight First Amendment Institute
  - Type: Essay
  - Cells: community-protocols, national-preferences, community-norms
  - Summary: Frames recommender ranking, collective response systems, and facilitated deliberation as one family of attention-allocation processes that can be designed to build understanding across divides.

- [Democratic Policy Development using Collective Dialogues and AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.02242)
  - Authors: Andrew Konya, Lisa Schirch, Colin Irwin, Aviv Ovadya
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: OpenAI
  - Type: Report
  - Cells: national-preferences, community-preferences, national-protocols
  - Summary: Combines AI-facilitated collective dialogues with bridging-based ranking to produce policy guidelines drawing 75-81 percent support across demographic splits in three American trials.

- [Generative Social Choice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01291)
  - Authors: Sara Fish, Paul Gölz, David C. Parkes, Ariel D. Procaccia, Gili Rusak, Itai Shapira, Manuel Wüthrich
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: EC 2024
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-preferences, community-preferences, national-preferences
  - Summary: Combines social-choice guarantees with language models to represent free-form public input rather than forcing people to choose among fixed options.

- [Opportunities and Risks of LLMs for Scalable Deliberation with Polis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.11932)
  - Authors: Christopher T. Small, Ivan Vendrov, Esin Durmus, Hadjar Homaei, Elizabeth Barry, Julien Cornebise, Ted Suzman, Deep Ganguli, Colin Megill
  - Year: 2023
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: community-protocols, national-preferences, community-preferences
  - Summary: Tests language models as facilitators, moderators, and summarisers in Polis deliberations, reporting gains in scale alongside context limits and risks to representativeness.

- [Reimagining Democracy for AI](https://www.journalofdemocracy.org/articles/reimagining-democracy-for-ai/)
  - Authors: Aviv Ovadya
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: Journal of Democracy
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-protocols, global-protocols, national-preferences
  - Summary: Identifies four shifts in democratic infrastructure — representative deliberations, AI augmentation, democracy-as-a-service, and platform democracy — as routes to transnational governance of AI.

- [Human-centred mechanism design with Democratic AI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-022-01383-x)
  - Authors: Raphael Koster, Jan Balaguer, Andrea Tacchetti, Ari Weinstein, Tina Zhu, Oliver Hauser, Duncan Williams, Lucy Campbell-Gillingham, Phoebe Thacker, Matthew Botvinick, Christopher Summerfield
  - Year: 2022
  - Venue: Nature Human Behaviour
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-preferences, group-incentives, community-incentives
  - Summary: Demonstrates a human-in-the-loop pipeline in which reinforcement learning designs a redistribution mechanism that participants then evaluate democratically.

- [Deliberation and epistemic democracy](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jebo.2021.02.020)
  - Authors: Huihui Ding, Marcus Pivato
  - Year: 2021
  - Venue: Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-preferences, community-preferences, national-preferences
  - Summary: Models when deliberation improves a group's collective judgement, showing the result depends on the decision rule, group size, and the presence of persuadable neutral agents.
