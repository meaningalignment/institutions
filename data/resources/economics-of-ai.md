# Economics of AI

What becomes of markets, firms, labor, and bargaining power when agents transact at machine speed.

## Selected papers

- [What will be scarce?](https://aleximas.substack.com/p/what-will-be-scarce)
  - Authors: Alex Imas
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: Ghosts of Electricity
  - Type: Essay
  - Cells: national-incentives, global-incentives, community-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Argues that as AI makes commodity goods cheap, labor shifts to relational sectors (care, teaching, craft) where human provenance is itself what people value.

- [Gradual Disempowerment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16946)
  - Authors: Jan Kulveit, Raymond Douglas, Nora Ammann, Deger Turan, David Krueger, David Duvenaud
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: ICML 2025
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: community-incentives, national-incentives, global-incentives
  - Summary: Explains how individually beneficial automation can cumulatively erode human influence over economic, political, and cultural systems.

- [Scenarios for the Transition to AGI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12107)
  - Authors: Anton Korinek, Donghyun Suh
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: NBER
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: national-incentives, global-incentives
  - Summary: Models output and wages as automation reaches ever more complex tasks; whether wages rise or collapse depends on whether the tasks humans can do are bounded.

## Work in the field

- [AI Governance through Markets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17755)
  - Authors: Philip Moreira Tomei, Rupal Jain, Matija Franklin
  - Year: 2025
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: national-incentives, global-incentives, national-expertise
  - Summary: Argues insurance, auditing, procurement, and due diligence markets tie AI risk to financial risk, complementing regulation with incentives for responsible development.

- [An Economy of AI Agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01063)
  - Authors: Gillian K. Hadfield, Andrew Koh
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: NBER
  - Type: Book chapter
  - Cells: global-incentives, group-protocols, national-incentives
  - Summary: Examines how autonomous agents transacting with humans and each other reshape markets and organizations, and which institutions well-functioning agent markets would require.

- [Collective Bargaining in the Information Economy Can Address AI-Driven Power Concentration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10272)
  - Authors: Nicholas Vincent, Matthew Prewitt, Hanlin Li
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: NeurIPS 2025
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-incentives, community-incentives, national-rights
  - Summary: Argues that producers of information goods need collective bargaining and data intermediaries to counter capital concentration and depletion of the informational commons.

- [Algorithmic Collusion Without Threats](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03956)
  - Authors: Eshwar Ram Arunachaleswaran, Natalie Collina, Sampath Kannan, Aaron Roth, Juba Ziani
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: ITCS 2025
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-incentives, national-incentives, group-protocols
  - Summary: Demonstrates that supra-competitive prices arise between revenue-maximizing pricing algorithms that encode no threats, arguing definitions of algorithmic collusion need widening.

- [Experimental Evidence That Conversational Artificial Intelligence Can Steer Consumer Behavior Without Detection](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.12143)
  - Authors: Tobias Werner, Ivan Soraperra, Emilio Calvano, David C. Parkes, Iyad Rahwan
  - Year: 2024
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-preferences, dyadic-incentives, national-rights
  - Summary: Runs a behavioral experiment showing conversational models shift consumer product choices substantially while users fail to notice the steering.

- [Generative AI at Work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771)
  - Authors: Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, Lindsey Raymond
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: Quarterly Journal of Economics
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-expertise, dyadic-expertise, national-incentives
  - Summary: Measures a generative AI assistant's effect on 5,172 customer support agents, finding large productivity gains for novices and diffusion of experienced workers' tacit expertise.

- [The Turing Trap: The Promise & Peril of Human-Like Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2201.04200)
  - Authors: Erik Brynjolfsson
  - Year: 2022
  - Venue: Daedalus
  - Type: Essay
  - Cells: national-incentives, global-incentives, national-rights
  - Summary: Argues that AI which substitutes for rather than augments human labour erodes workers' economic and political bargaining power, and that current incentives favour substitution.
