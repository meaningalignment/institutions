# Economics of AI

What becomes of markets, firms, labor, and bargaining power when agents transact at machine speed.

## Selected papers

- [The Computer and the Market](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Computer_and_the_Market)
  - Authors: Oskar Lange
  - Year: 1967
  - Venue: Socialism, Capitalism and Economic Growth (Cambridge University Press)
  - Type: Book chapter
  - Cells: national-incentives, national-preferences, global-incentives
  - Summary: Argues that an electronic computer could solve the allocation equations a market solves, treating the market as a slow computing device that planning could replace.

- [The Use of Knowledge in Society](https://www.econlib.org/library/Essays/hykKnw.html)
  - Authors: Friedrich Hayek
  - Year: 1945
  - Venue: American Economic Review
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-incentives, national-expertise
  - Summary: Argues that the knowledge needed to allocate resources is dispersed and local, and that prices coordinate it without any planner ever holding it.

- [Multitask Principal-Agent Analyses: Incentive Contracts, Asset Ownership, and Job Design](https://doi.org/10.1093/jleo/7.special_issue.24)
  - Authors: Bengt Holmström, Paul Milgrom
  - Year: 1991
  - Venue: Journal of Law, Economics, and Organization
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-incentives, group-incentives
  - Summary: Shows that when an agent splits effort across tasks and some are hard to measure, strong incentives on the measurable ones pull effort from the rest, so optimal incentives are often weak.

- [GPTs are GPTs: An Early Look at the Labor Market Impact Potential of Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10130)
  - Authors: Tyna Eloundou, Sam Manning, Pamela Mishkin, Daniel Rock
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: Science
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-incentives, national-expertise
  - Summary: Rates US occupations' tasks for exposure to large language models and finds that most workers have at least some exposed tasks, with higher-wage jobs more exposed.

- [Expertise](https://www.nber.org/papers/w33941)
  - Authors: David Autor, Neil Thompson
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: NBER
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: national-expertise, group-expertise, national-incentives
  - Summary: Argues that automation's effect on a job depends on which tasks it removes: taking away inexpert tasks raises the expertise a job demands and its wage, while taking away expert tasks does the reverse.

- [The Simple Macroeconomics of AI](https://www.nber.org/papers/w32487)
  - Authors: Daron Acemoglu
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Economic Policy
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-incentives, global-incentives
  - Summary: Builds aggregate effects up from task-level cost savings and estimates modest productivity gains from AI over the next decade, well under one percent of total factor productivity.

- [Generative AI at Work](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.11771)
  - Authors: Erik Brynjolfsson, Danielle Li, Lindsey Raymond
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: Quarterly Journal of Economics
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-expertise, dyadic-expertise, national-incentives
  - Summary: Measures a generative AI assistant's effect on 5,172 customer support agents, finding large productivity gains for novices and diffusion of experienced workers' tacit expertise.
