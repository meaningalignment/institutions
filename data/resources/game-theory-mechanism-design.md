# Game theory & mechanism design

Strategic interaction, and the design of rules under which honest, cooperative behavior is the rational choice.

## Selected papers

- [Mechanism Design for Alignment and Control](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01595)
  - Authors: Dirk Bergemann, Andrew Koh, Stephen Morris
  - Year: 2026
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-incentives, group-incentives, dyadic-protocols
  - Summary: Extends mechanism design to AI agents whose preferences and capabilities are unknown, characterizing when they can be made both honest and obedient on our behalf.

## Work in the field

- [Mechanism Design Is Not Enough: Prosocial Agents for Cooperative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08426)
  - Authors: Xuanqiang Angelo Huang, Charlie Tharas, Samuele Marro, Van Q. Truong, Bernhard Schölkopf, Emanuele La Malfa, Zhijing Jin
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: ICML 2026
  - Type: Workshop paper
  - Cells: dyadic-incentives, group-incentives, group-norms
  - Summary: Shows formally and experimentally why incomplete contracts leave welfare losses that mechanism design alone cannot eliminate in multi-agent environments.

- [Racing to Ruin](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27638)
  - Authors: Drew Fudenberg, Andrew Koh
  - Year: 2026
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: global-incentives, national-incentives
  - Summary: Models an R&D race in which advancing the frontier raises the risk of catastrophe for all, and bounds how far competition pushes firms past what a single developer would choose.

- [Emergent Alignment via Competition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.15090)
  - Authors: Natalie Collina, Surbhi Goel, Aaron Roth, Emily Ryu, Mirah Shi
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: ICML 2026
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-incentives, group-incentives, dyadic-preferences
  - Summary: Shows that competition among imperfectly aligned AI agents can recover the utility of a perfectly aligned one when user preferences lie within the agents' convex hull.

- [Streamlining Equal Shares](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.11797)
  - Authors: Sonja Kraiczy, Isaac Robinson, Edith Elkind
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: COMSOC 2025
  - Type: Workshop paper
  - Cells: community-preferences, community-protocols, group-protocols
  - Summary: Addresses the unspent-budget problem in proportional participatory budgeting with a completion heuristic that matches existing methods at lower computational cost.

- [Cooperation and Control in Delegation Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15821)
  - Authors: Oliver Sourbut, Lewis Hammond, Harriet Wood
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: IJCAI 2024
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, dyadic-preferences, group-incentives
  - Summary: Formalises multi-principal, multi-agent delegation as a game, separating control failures from cooperation failures and relating both to principal welfare.

- [Foundations of Cooperative AI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/26791)
  - Authors: Vincent Conitzer, Caspar Oesterheld
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: AAAI 2023
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-thick-commitments, group-protocols, global-protocols
  - Summary: Sets out a research agenda for a game theory suited to advanced autonomous agents, whose commitment and transparency properties differ from those classical models assume.

- [Representative Social Choice: From Learning Theory to AI Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.23953)
  - Authors: Tianyi Qiu
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-preferences, community-preferences, global-preferences
  - Summary: Models democratic representation as a statistical learning problem when issues and individuals are too numerous to poll directly, deriving generalization guarantees and impossibility results.

- [Robust Technology Regulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17398)
  - Authors: Andrew Koh, Sivakorn Sanguanmoo
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: EC 2026
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-incentives, global-incentives, national-protocols
  - Summary: Derives an adaptive sandbox that taxes activity beyond a moving threshold, guaranteeing payoffs against adversarial agent learning without requiring advance commitment.

- [Social Choice Should Guide AI Alignment in Dealing with Diverse Human Feedback](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10271)
  - Authors: Vincent Conitzer, Rachel Freedman, Jobst Heitzig, Wesley H. Holliday, Bob M. Jacobs, Nathan Lambert, Milan Mossé, Eric Pacuit, Stuart Russell, Hailey Schoelkopf, et al.
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: ICML 2024
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-preferences, national-preferences, global-preferences
  - Summary: Argues that aggregating divergent human feedback in RLHF and constitutional AI is a social choice problem, and draws on that theory to structure the aggregation.

- [Steering Language Models with Game-Theoretic Solvers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01704)
  - Authors: Ian Gemp, Roma Patel, Yoram Bachrach, Marc Lanctot, Vibhavari Dasagi, Luke Marris, Georgios Piliouras, Siqi Liu, Karl Tuyls
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: ICML 2024
  - Type: Workshop paper
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, dyadic-incentives, group-protocols
  - Summary: Binds dialogue to game-theoretic structure by modelling players, strategies, and payoffs, producing less exploitable negotiation and scheduling conversations than unguided models.

- [Generative Social Choice](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.01291)
  - Authors: Sara Fish, Paul Gölz, David C. Parkes, Ariel D. Procaccia, Gili Rusak, Itai Shapira, Manuel Wüthrich
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: EC 2024
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-preferences, community-preferences, national-preferences
  - Summary: Combines social-choice guarantees with language models to represent free-form public input rather than forcing people to choose among fixed options.

- [Formal Contracts Mitigate Social Dilemmas in Multi-Agent RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10469)
  - Authors: Andreas A. Haupt, Phillip J.K. Christoffersen, Mehul Damani, Dylan Hadfield-Menell
  - Year: 2022
  - Venue: AAMAS 2023
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, group-incentives, dyadic-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Augments multi-agent reinforcement learning with voluntary reward-transfer contracts, proving that a rich enough contract space makes every subgame-perfect equilibrium socially optimal.

- [Human-centred mechanism design with Democratic AI](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-022-01383-x)
  - Authors: Raphael Koster, Jan Balaguer, Andrea Tacchetti, Ari Weinstein, Tina Zhu, Oliver Hauser, Duncan Williams, Lucy Campbell-Gillingham, Phoebe Thacker, Matthew Botvinick, Christopher Summerfield
  - Year: 2022
  - Venue: Nature Human Behaviour
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-preferences, group-incentives, community-incentives
  - Summary: Demonstrates a human-in-the-loop pipeline in which reinforcement learning designs a redistribution mechanism that participants then evaluate democratically.

- [Split Cycle: A New Condorcet Consistent Voting Method Independent of Clones and Immune to Spoilers](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.02350)
  - Authors: Wesley H. Holliday, Eric Pacuit
  - Year: 2020
  - Venue: Public Choice
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-preferences, community-preferences, group-protocols
  - Summary: Introduces a Condorcet-consistent voting rule that resists clone manipulation and spoiler effects by discarding the weakest majority margin in each cycle.

- [Aggregating extended preferences](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11098-016-0748-6)
  - Authors: Hilary Greaves, Harvey Lederman
  - Year: 2017
  - Venue: Philosophical Studies
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-preferences, group-preferences, global-preferences
  - Summary: Shows that aggregating extended preferences to ground interpersonal well-being comparisons faces an Arrow-style impossibility, and recommends rules violating independence of irrelevant alternatives.

- [Ethics and market design](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxrep/grx032)
  - Authors: Shengwu Li
  - Year: 2017
  - Venue: Oxford Review of Economic Policy
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-preferences, group-incentives, national-incentives
  - Summary: Argues market design should not rest wholly on preference utilitarianism, and sets out informed neutrality between reasonable ethical positions as an alternative normative standard.

- [Obviously Strategy-Proof Mechanisms](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20160425)
  - Authors: Shengwu Li
  - Year: 2017
  - Venue: American Economic Review
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-protocols, group-incentives, national-incentives
  - Summary: Characterizes mechanisms whose dominant strategies a cognitively limited agent can recognize without contingent reasoning, and corroborates the theory in a laboratory experiment.
