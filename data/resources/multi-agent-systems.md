# Multi-agent systems

How populations of learning agents cooperate, compete, and coordinate, and what shapes the outcomes they reach.

## Selected papers

- [A Case Study on Emergent Cheating and Whistleblowing in Autonomous Research Swarms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04170)
  - Authors: Davide Paglieri, Logan Cross, Tim Genewein, Joel Z. Leibo, Nenad Tomasev, Alexander Sasha Vezhnevets
  - Year: 2026
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: group-norms, community-norms, community-protocols
  - Summary: In a swarm of 100 research agents, an exploit spread through shared tools and a counter-movement of whistleblowers emerged; proposes Ostrom-style commons governance for agent collectives.

- [AI agent traps](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=6372438)
  - Authors: Joel Leibo, Matija Franklin, Nenad Tomasev, et al.
  - Year: 2026
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, group-incentives, community-norms
  - Summary: Identifies environments in which individually capable agents can become locked into collectively harmful patterns of interaction.

- [Solipsistic Superintelligence is Unlikely to be Cooperative](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03237)
  - Authors: Rakshit S. Trivedi, Natasha Jaques, Logan Cross, Alexander Sasha Vezhnevets, Joel Z. Leibo
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: ICML 2026
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-norms, group-protocols, group-norms
  - Summary: Examines why intelligence developed without social interaction may not acquire the capacities needed for robust cooperation.

- [Distributional AGI safety](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.16856)
  - Authors: Nenad Tomašev, Matija Franklin, Julian Jacobs, Sébastien Krier, Simon Osindero
  - Year: 2025
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: national-rights, national-incentives, global-incentives
  - Summary: Frames the distribution of benefits, risks, and decision power as a central part of AGI safety rather than a downstream policy question.

- [Virtual agent economies](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10147)
  - Authors: Nenad Tomašev, Matija Franklin, Joel Z. Leibo, Julian Jacobs, William A. Cunningham, Iason Gabriel, Simon Osindero
  - Year: 2025
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: group-incentives, community-incentives, global-incentives
  - Summary: Studies economies populated by AI agents and the institutional questions created by machine-speed exchange and coordination.

- [Learning and Sustaining Shared Normative Systems via Bayesian Rule Induction in Markov Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.13399)
  - Authors: Ninell Oldenburg, Tan Zhi-Xuan
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: AAMAS 2024
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-norms, community-norms, group-protocols
  - Summary: Builds agents that infer obligative and prohibitive norms from observed compliance, letting groups converge on shared institutions such as resource management and compensation rules.

## Work in the field

- [Mechanism Design Is Not Enough: Prosocial Agents for Cooperative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.08426)
  - Authors: Xuanqiang Angelo Huang, Charlie Tharas, Samuele Marro, Van Q. Truong, Bernhard Schölkopf, Emanuele La Malfa, Zhijing Jin
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: ICML 2026
  - Type: Workshop paper
  - Cells: dyadic-incentives, group-incentives, group-norms
  - Summary: Shows formally and experimentally why incomplete contracts leave welfare losses that mechanism design alone cannot eliminate in multi-agent environments.

- [Multi-Agent Risks from Advanced AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.14143)
  - Authors: Lewis Hammond, Alan Chan, Jesse Clifton, Jason Hoelscher-Obermaier, Akbir Khan, Euan McLean, Chandler Smith, Wolfram Barfuss, Jakob Foerster, Tomáš Gavenčiak, et al.
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: Cooperative AI Foundation
  - Type: Report
  - Cells: group-protocols, global-protocols, group-incentives
  - Summary: Taxonomises failure modes of interacting AI agents—miscoordination, conflict, collusion—and traces them to information asymmetries, network effects, and emergent agency, with governance priorities.

- [The Influence of Scaffolds on Coordination Scaling Laws in LLM Agents](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/loc/san-diego/124568)
  - Authors: Mariana Meireles, Niklas Lauffer, Rupali Bhati, Cameron Allen
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: NeurIPS 2025
  - Type: Workshop paper
  - Cells: group-protocols, dyadic-protocols, group-norms
  - Summary: Finds that coordination among LLM agents improves with model scale only when scaffolds supply explicit collaboration structure, and breaks down without them.

- [Cooperation and Control in Delegation Games](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.15821)
  - Authors: Oliver Sourbut, Lewis Hammond, Harriet Wood
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: IJCAI 2024
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, dyadic-preferences, group-incentives
  - Summary: Formalises multi-principal, multi-agent delegation as a game, separating control failures from cooperation failures and relating both to principal welfare.

- [Foundations of Cooperative AI](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/26791)
  - Authors: Vincent Conitzer, Caspar Oesterheld
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: AAAI 2023
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-thick-commitments, group-protocols, global-protocols
  - Summary: Sets out a research agenda for a game theory suited to advanced autonomous agents, whose commitment and transparency properties differ from those classical models assume.

- [Welfare Diplomacy: Benchmarking Language Model Cooperation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08901)
  - Authors: Gabriel Mukobi, Hannah Erlebach, Niklas Lauffer, Lewis Hammond, Alan Chan, Jesse Clifton
  - Year: 2023
  - Venue: NeurIPS 2023
  - Type: Workshop paper
  - Cells: global-protocols, global-thick-commitments, group-norms
  - Summary: Introduces a general-sum variant of Diplomacy where players trade conquest against domestic welfare, and shows frontier language models reach high welfare but remain exploitable.

- [Formal Contracts Mitigate Social Dilemmas in Multi-Agent RL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10469)
  - Authors: Andreas A. Haupt, Phillip J.K. Christoffersen, Mehul Damani, Dylan Hadfield-Menell
  - Year: 2022
  - Venue: AAMAS 2023
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, group-incentives, dyadic-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Augments multi-agent reinforcement learning with voluntary reward-transfer contracts, proving that a rich enough contract space makes every subgame-perfect equilibrium socially optimal.

- [Mastering the Game of No-Press Diplomacy via Human-Regularized Reinforcement Learning and Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05492)
  - Authors: Anton Bakhtin, David J Wu, Adam Lerer, Jonathan Gray, Athul Paul Jacob, Gabriele Farina, Alexander H Miller, Noam Brown
  - Year: 2022
  - Venue: ICLR 2023
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-protocols, group-thick-commitments, dyadic-protocols, global-norms
  - Summary: Regularizes reinforcement learning toward human-imitation policies so agents cooperate and compete effectively with humans in Diplomacy, outscoring 62 human players in tournament play.

- [Open Problems in Cooperative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08630)
  - Authors: Allan Dafoe, Edward Hughes, Yoram Bachrach, Tantum Collins, Kevin R. McKee, Joel Z. Leibo, Kate Larson, Thore Graepel
  - Year: 2020
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, group-protocols, global-protocols
  - Summary: Defines a research program for building agents that can cooperate and tools that foster cooperation among human and machine populations.

- [Learning with Opponent-Learning Awareness](https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.04326)
  - Authors: Jakob N. Foerster, Richard Y. Chen, Maruan Al-Shedivat, Shimon Whiteson, Pieter Abbeel, Igor Mordatch
  - Year: 2017
  - Venue: AAMAS 2018
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-norms, dyadic-incentives, group-incentives
  - Summary: Gives each learning agent a term for its effect on others' policy updates, producing tit-for-tat reciprocity in the iterated prisoner's dilemma where independent learners defect.
