# Negotiation & international cooperation

How parties with divergent interests reach and keep binding agreements, whether they are people, states, or agents.

## Selected papers


## Work in the field

- [CoopEval: Benchmarking Cooperation-Sustaining Mechanisms and LLM Agents in Social Dilemmas](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/tewolde26a.html)
  - Authors: Emanuel Tewolde, X. Zhang, D. Guzman Piedrahita, Vincent Conitzer, Zhijing Jin
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: ICML 2026
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-protocols, group-incentives, dyadic-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Finds that LLM agents consistently defect in single-shot social dilemmas, then compares repetition, reputation, mediation, and contracts as ways to sustain cooperation among them.

- [Evaluating Rational Contracting in Natural Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10475)
  - Authors: B. Sajja, Max Kleiman-Weiner, R. Zimmermann, Tan Zhi-Xuan
  - Year: 2026
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, dyadic-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Frontier LLM agents negotiate efficient contracts when uncertainty is low, but often breach them against cooperative partners. A first benchmark that tests compliance with negotiated commitments, with a good survey of current work on AI bargaining.

- [Racing to the Precipice: A Model of Artificial Intelligence Development](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-015-0590-y)
  - Authors: Stuart Armstrong, Nick Bostrom, Carl Shulman
  - Year: 2016
  - Venue: AI & Society
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-incentives
  - Summary: In a race to build powerful AI, more teams means less safety effort, and more information about rivals' capabilities can increase risk.

- [The AGI Race and Existential Risk](https://www.nber.org/papers/w35276)
  - Authors: Ethan Bueno de Mesquita, Wioletta Dziuda, Mattias Polborn
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: NBER Working Paper 35276
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: global-incentives, national-incentives
  - Summary: Updates the racing model in light of what we now observe. Firms split resources between speed and safety; fragmentation raises risk, and above a critical market size firms race even when reaching AGI has negative expected value.

- [International Governance of Civilian AI: A Jurisdictional Certification Approach](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.15514)
  - Authors: Robert Trager, et al.
  - Year: 2023
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: global-protocols, global-incentives, global-expertise
  - Summary: Proposes an international AI organization that certifies jurisdictions, rather than firms, against safety standards, enforced through import restrictions and chip export controls. Draws on existing analogues such as ICAO, IMO, and FATF.

- [Verifying International Agreements on AI: Six Layers of Verification for Rules on Large-Scale AI Development and Deployment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.15916)
  - Authors: Mauricio Baker, et al.
  - Year: 2025
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: global-protocols, global-expertise
  - Summary: Verification is the precondition for most cooperation theory. Sets out what would need to exist before compute-based agreements could be enforced.

- [Weaponized Interdependence: How Global Economic Networks Shape State Coercion](https://doi.org/10.1162/isec_a_00351)
  - Authors: Henry Farrell, Abraham L. Newman
  - Year: 2019
  - Venue: International Security
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-incentives, global-rights
  - Summary: States that control central nodes in global networks can use them to monitor others and cut them off, turning interdependence into leverage. The AI economy rests on data centers, undersea cables, and rare earths, all inside some state's territory. See also the authors' book Underground Empire.

- [Incomplete Contracting and AI Alignment](https://doi.org/10.1145/3306618.3314250)
  - Authors: Dylan Hadfield-Menell, Gillian K. Hadfield
  - Year: 2019
  - Venue: AIES 2019
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-incentives, dyadic-norms, community-norms
  - Summary: Bridges contract theory, law and economics, and alignment. Treats alignment as incomplete contracting and argues that human contracts work because outside institutions, such as law and culture, supply the missing terms.

- [AI Can Help Humans Find Common Ground in Democratic Deliberation](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adq2852)
  - Authors: Michael Henry Tessler, Michiel A. Bakker, et al.
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Science
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-preferences, community-preferences, community-norms
  - Summary: The strongest existing evidence that AI can do a mediator's job. An AI that drafted and revised group statements, respecting the majority while accounting for dissent, produced statements preferred to those of human mediators.

- [The Coasean Singularity? Demand, Supply, and Market Design with AI Agents](https://www.nber.org/papers/w34468)
  - Authors: Peyman Shahidi, Gabriel Rusak, Benjamin S. Manning, Andrey Fradkin, John J. Horton
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: NBER Working Paper 34468
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, dyadic-incentives, community-incentives
  - Summary: AI agents lower search, communication, and contracting costs and widen the set of feasible market designs, but they also add new frictions, such as congestion and price obfuscation.

- [Open Problems in Cooperative AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.08630)
  - Authors: Allan Dafoe, Edward Hughes, Yoram Bachrach, Tantum Collins, Kevin R. McKee, Joel Z. Leibo, Kate Larson, Thore Graepel
  - Year: 2020
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, group-protocols, global-protocols
  - Summary: An influential research agenda for cooperative AI, organized around four capabilities agents need: understanding, communication, commitment, and institutions.

## Foundations

- [An Essay on Bargaining](https://www.jstor.org/stable/1805498)
  - Authors: Thomas C. Schelling
  - Year: 1956
  - Venue: American Economic Review
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, dyadic-thick-commitments, global-incentives
  - Summary: The starting point of modern bargaining theory. Shows how parties gain bargaining power by credibly binding themselves.

- [War as a Commitment Problem](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020818306060061)
  - Authors: Robert Powell
  - Year: 2006
  - Venue: International Organization
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-incentives, global-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Rapid shifts in power make promises less credible, so actors defect even when a deal would leave both better off. AGI is an extreme case of rapid power shift, so labs and states might reject agreements they would otherwise prefer. Builds on Fearon's "Rationalist Explanations for War" (1995).

- [Program Equilibrium](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geb.2004.02.002)
  - Authors: Moshe Tennenholtz
  - Year: 2004
  - Venue: Games and Economic Behavior
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, dyadic-thick-commitments
  - Summary: The origin of open-source game theory and of the hope that AI agents can commit in ways humans cannot. If players submit programs that can read each other's code, mutual cooperation can be an equilibrium even in a one-shot prisoner's dilemma.

- [Diplomacy and Domestic Politics: The Logic of Two-Level Games](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0020818300027697)
  - Authors: Robert D. Putnam
  - Year: 1988
  - Venue: International Organization
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-norms, national-preferences
  - Summary: Negotiators play two games at once, with the foreign counterparty and with domestic constituents, so tighter domestic constraints can strengthen a negotiator's hand. Relevant to US–China bargains.

- [Bargaining, Enforcement, and International Cooperation](https://doi.org/10.1162/002081898753162820)
  - Authors: James D. Fearon
  - Year: 1998
  - Venue: International Organization
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-incentives, global-protocols
  - Summary: States must agree on terms before they can enforce them, and the long shadow of the future that eases enforcement also rewards hard bargaining and costly delay. The features that would make an AGI agreement stable, long horizons and high stakes, may also delay reaching it.

- [Arguing and Bargaining in Two Constituent Assemblies](https://scholarship.law.upenn.edu/jcl/vol2/iss2/1)
  - Authors: Jon Elster
  - Year: 2000
  - Venue: University of Pennsylvania Journal of Constitutional Law
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: national-rights, national-preferences
  - Summary: Compares the constitutional assemblies of Philadelphia (1787) and Paris (1789–91), showing how public argument pushed self-interested delegates toward impartial positions: "the civilizing force of hypocrisy." Grand bargains tend to follow trauma and passion more than calm reason, which may be what a global AGI agreement takes.

- [Incomplete Contracts and the Theory of the Firm](https://doi.org/10.1093/oxfordjournals.jleo.a036940)
  - Authors: Oliver D. Hart
  - Year: 1988
  - Venue: Journal of Law, Economics, and Organization
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-rights, group-incentives
  - Summary: Because contracts cannot anticipate every future state of the world, whoever holds residual control rights when the unforeseen happens shapes the incentives of firms.

- [Incomplete Contracts and Strategic Ambiguity](https://bernheim.people.stanford.edu/publications/incomplete-contracts-and-strategic-ambiguity)
  - Authors: B. Douglas Bernheim, Michael D. Whinston
  - Year: 1998
  - Venue: American Economic Review
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-protocols, dyadic-incentives
  - Summary: Deliberate vagueness can be optimal, because specifying terms lets parties exploit them. A check on the intuition that more precise AI specifications are always better.

- [Hard and Soft Law in International Governance](https://doi.org/10.1162/002081800551280)
  - Authors: Kenneth W. Abbott, Duncan Snidal
  - Year: 2000
  - Venue: International Organization
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-norms, global-protocols, global-rights
  - Summary: International agreements vary in obligation, precision, and delegation. Low-precision soft law, which is what all AI governance currently is, can be the better design under uncertainty and high sovereignty costs. See also Koremenos, Lipson and Snidal, "The Rational Design of International Institutions" (2001).

- [The Problem of Social Cost](https://doi.org/10.1086/466560)
  - Authors: Ronald H. Coase
  - Year: 1960
  - Venue: Journal of Law and Economics
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: community-rights, dyadic-incentives
  - Summary: If transaction costs are low, parties bargain to efficient outcomes whatever the initial assignment of rights, so institutions exist to lower transaction costs. The agentic economy is a bet that AI makes this idealization real.

- [Efficient Mechanisms for Bilateral Trading](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531%2883%2990048-0)
  - Authors: Roger B. Myerson, Mark A. Satterthwaite
  - Year: 1983
  - Venue: Journal of Economic Theory
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-incentives, dyadic-protocols
  - Summary: With privately known valuations, no voluntary trading mechanism can guarantee efficient trade without an outside subsidy. A hard bound that AI bargaining may not remove, and the reason AI-enabled verification of private information matters.
