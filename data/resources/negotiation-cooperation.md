## Work in the field

- [Using Collective Dialogues and AI to Find Common Ground Between Israeli and Palestinian Peacebuilders](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01769)
  - Authors: Andrew Konya, Luke Thorburn, Wasim Almasri, Oded Adomi Leshem, Ariel D. Procaccia, Lisa Schirch, Michiel A. Bakker
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: FAccT 2025
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-preferences, community-preferences, global-thick-commitments, global-norms
  - Summary: Applies language models, social choice methods, and collective dialogues with 138 peacebuilders to produce shared statements endorsed by at least 84 percent on each side.

- [Mastering the Game of No-Press Diplomacy via Human-Regularized Reinforcement Learning and Planning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.05492)
  - Authors: Anton Bakhtin, David J Wu, Adam Lerer, Jonathan Gray, Athul Paul Jacob, Gabriele Farina, Alexander H Miller, Noam Brown
  - Year: 2022
  - Venue: ICLR 2023
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: group-protocols, group-thick-commitments, dyadic-protocols, global-norms
  - Summary: Regularizes reinforcement learning toward human-imitation policies so agents cooperate and compete effectively with humans in Diplomacy, outscoring 62 human players in tournament play.

- [The Politics of Precedent in International Law: A Social Network Application](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055414000276)
  - Authors: Krzysztof J. Pelc
  - Year: 2014
  - Venue: American Political Science Review
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-protocols, global-rights, global-norms
  - Summary: Shows that states litigate to shape legal precedent even where precedent is formally non-binding, treating case law as a contested institutional resource.
