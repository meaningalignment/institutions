# Philosophy of AI

What values are, how agents reason with plural ones, and what mind, meaning, and agency amount to in artificial systems.

## Selected papers

- [Solipsistic Superintelligence is Unlikely to be Cooperative](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03237)
  - Authors: Rakshit S. Trivedi, Natasha Jaques, Logan Cross, Alexander Sasha Vezhnevets, Joel Z. Leibo
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: ICML 2026
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-norms, group-protocols, group-norms
  - Summary: Examines why intelligence developed without social interaction may not acquire the capacities needed for robust cooperation.

- [Discerning What Matters: A Multi-Dimensional Assessment of Moral Competence in LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.13082)
  - Authors: Daniel Kilov, Caroline Hendy, Secil Yanik Guyot, Aaron J. Snoswell, Seth Lazar
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: IASEAI 2026
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-expertise, dyadic-thick-commitments, national-expertise
  - Summary: Evaluates moral competence as a collection of distinguishable capacities rather than a single benchmark score.

- [Beyond Preferences in AI Alignment](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16984)
  - Authors: Tan Zhi-Xuan, Micah Carroll, Matija Franklin, Hal Ashton
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Philosophical Studies
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-preferences, group-preferences, group-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Shows why preference satisfaction is too thin a target for alignment and develops a broader account of what AI systems should respond to.

## Work in the field

- [Conversational Alignment with Artificial Intelligence in Context](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.22907)
  - Authors: Rachel Katharine Sterken, James Ravi Kirkpatrick
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: Philosophical Perspectives
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-norms, dyadic-protocols, dyadic-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Proposes the CONTEXT-ALIGN framework for judging whether AI agents handle context and common ground as human conversational norms require, and argues current architectures constrain this.

- [The Philosophic Turn for AI Agents: Replacing centralized digital rhetoric with decentralized truth-seeking](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.18601)
  - Authors: Philipp Koralus
  - Year: 2025
  - Venue: Mind & Society
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-expertise, dyadic-preferences, national-preferences
  - Summary: Argues that nudge-based AI decision support erodes autonomy at scale, and proposes Socratic, decentralized truth-seeking systems that leave judgment with the user.

- [Are Language Models More Like Libraries or Like Librarians? Bibliotechnism, the Novel Reference Problem, and the Attitudes of LLMs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04854)
  - Authors: Harvey Lederman, Kyle Mahowald
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: TACL
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-expertise, dyadic-norms, community-expertise
  - Summary: Defends language models as cultural transmission technologies, then argues novel reference gives grounds for attributing beliefs and intentions without consciousness or general intelligence.

- [Automatic Authorities: Power and AI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.05990)
  - Authors: Seth Lazar
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: MIT Press
  - Type: Book chapter
  - Cells: community-rights, national-rights, national-norms
  - Summary: Analyzes automated systems as exercises of power and argues that legitimate use requires procedural authority, not only beneficial outcomes.

- [Two Types of AI Existential Risk: Decisive and Accumulative](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.07836)
  - Authors: Atoosa Kasirzadeh
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Philosophical Studies
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: global-rights, national-thick-commitments, global-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Contrasts sudden takeover scenarios with an accumulative pathway in which interlocking AI disruptions erode economic and political resilience until collapse becomes irreversible.

- [Contextual Trust](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08900)
  - Authors: Ryan Othniel Kearns
  - Year: 2023
  - Type: Preprint
  - Cells: dyadic-thick-commitments, dyadic-expertise, dyadic-norms
  - Summary: Defines trust as action- and context-sensitive, then maps transparency and explainability requirements for opaque AI systems onto those contextual parameters.

- [Hard Choices and Hard Limits for Artificial Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.07852)
  - Authors: Bryce Goodman
  - Year: 2021
  - Venue: AIES 2021
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Cells: dyadic-preferences, national-rights, dyadic-thick-commitments
  - Summary: Argues that where alternatives stand on a par rather than ranking against each other, AI cannot and should not settle the choice for a decision-maker.

- [Ethical learning, natural and artificial](https://books.google.com/books?hl=en&lr=&id=2yT3DwAAQBAJ&oi=fnd&pg=PA45)
  - Authors: Peter Railton
  - Year: 2020
  - Venue: Oxford University Press
  - Type: Book chapter
  - Cells: dyadic-norms, community-norms, group-norms
  - Summary: Reframes machine ethics around building systems with a capacity for ethical learning, drawing on developmental psychology's account of how infants acquire moral competence.
