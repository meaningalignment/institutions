# Political philosophy

What makes power legitimate, what freedom and equality require, and who answers to whom, now that AI systems increasingly exercise and mediate power.

## Selected papers

- [Legitimacy, Authority, and Democratic Duties of Explanation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.08628)
  - Authors: Seth Lazar
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Oxford Studies in Political Philosophy
  - Type: Book chapter
  - Summary: Argues that governing power is legitimate only if it can be publicly explained to those it governs, so opaque computational systems put the legitimacy of the institutions that use them in question.

- [Toward a Theory of Justice for Artificial Intelligence](https://doi.org/10.1162/daed_a_01911)
  - Authors: Iason Gabriel
  - Year: 2022
  - Venue: Daedalus
  - Type: Essay
  - Summary: Argues that AI systems now form part of Rawls's basic structure of society, so they fall under the same demands of public justification, equal rights, and fair distribution as other major institutions.

- [Empowering Digital Democracy](https://doi.org/10.1017/S1537592724000409)
  - Authors: Roberta Fischli, James Muldoon
  - Year: 2024
  - Venue: Perspectives on Politics
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Summary: Argues that democratizing the digital sphere takes more than regulating platforms, and sets out institutions through which citizens could exercise collective control over digital infrastructure.

- [Agents, Alignment, and the Many Faces of Autonomy](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11023-026-09786-9)
  - Authors: Roberta Fischli, Matija Franklin, Arianna Manzini, Iason Gabriel
  - Year: 2026
  - Venue: Minds and Machines
  - Type: Peer-reviewed
  - Summary: Shows that respecting user autonomy can mean acting on stated wishes, building the user's capabilities, or protecting their choices, and works through how an AI agent should act when these conflict.
